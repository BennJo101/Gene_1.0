      Battery and power

      The pack sits in the dome just above the twin servos, the lowest place a horizontal 2S2P pack fits. It is held in a tray that screws to pads on top of both servo brackets. A deck on top carries the two 5 V converters and the charger board, so the pack, deck and electronics can be built on the bench as one unit.

      Assembly

      Fit order matters

      The tray can't pass between the two servos, which leave only a 34 mm gap. Build the pack, tray and deck on the bench first. Lift the unit straight up through the open bottom of the skull into the dome (checked: no contact on the way up). Then fit both drive brackets with their servos, lower the tray onto the bracket pads, and drive 4 × M3×12 self-tappers up through the pads from underneath. The Pi shelf, crown insert, jaw and neck go in after that.

      The pack stays in the head. It needs a charge port through the skull, which isn't cut yet.

      Changes in this round

      New

      GPIO flashlight, and a cover for the GPIO well

      gpio_flashlight.stl is one printed part. A saddle on the crown insert carries a standard 2×20 female header, fitted upside down, that plugs onto the crown pins. A tongue reaches down into the well to hold the header, which is glued in with a 0.3 mm gap. A strap runs forward over the brow to a lamp pod in the middle of the forehead. The pod holds a 20 mm LED star behind a 20 mm 30° optic, with the driver in a cavity behind them, and it points straight ahead. The wires run in a channel under the strap. The saddle takes the two front crown screws (M3×20 socket heads instead of the countersunk M3×12), so knocks don't lever on the pins. That means it replaces the bridge handle rather than fitting with it. It rides 0.2 mm off the crown and brow and stays out of both cameras' views. Pick the Flashlight expression to see the lamp.

      Electrically it uses 3 of the 40 pins: 5 V, ground and GPIO 12 (PWM, for dimming). A logic-level MOSFET switches the LED through a 10 Ω resistor, for about 200 mA and 60–70 lm. That's roughly 250 lux at 1 m, against about 2 lux from both eye screens. Which crown pin is which depends on how the ribbon is folded, so find 5 V and ground with a meter before soldering.

      gpio_cover.stl closes the well when nothing is plugged in. It sits flush with the crown, its skirts straddle the pins on all four sides, and four small crush ribs hold it in. A notch at the rear end takes a fingernail or a small screwdriver to lift it out.

      New

      Neck that turns on a slip ring

      neck.stl is one part: a yoke that bolts up into the skull's rear rim with 5 × M3 (the rim is a 7.5 mm vertical wall, and skull.stl has the pilots), and a 44 mm tube hanging from it on the axis X 0, Y +20. The tube's 26 mm bore is the cable hole into the head.

      neck_base.stl bolts to your body or stand with 4 × M4. It holds two 6808 bearings (40×52×7) 20 mm apart. The head's weight sits on the top bearing through a step on the tube, and neck_collar.stl clamps under the bottom bearing so the head can't lift off. A capsule slip ring bolts to the base floor and reaches up into the tube bore: its fixed wires leave through the bottom, and the rotating wires run up the tube into the head. The head can turn without a stop.

      The chin swings back under the head as the mouth opens, so the neck limits the jaw. It clears by 12 mm at 30° (the full speaking range) and first touches the front of the neck base at 35.5°. With the neck fitted, set the jaw servo limit to 33°. The base's flange is flat at the front to get that far.

      New

      Crown feature bay, with the Pi's GPIO brought to the top

      crown_insert.stl fills the top slot flush with the dome. Its top follows the measured rim heights on both sides of the slot, not a fitted sphere. It drops in from above onto a 4 mm ledge the skull now has round the underside of the slot (added in v5, kept in skull.stl), and 4 × M3×12 countersunk screws go down into bosses under that ledge. Nothing needs reaching from inside.

      In the flattest middle of the crown, the male plug of a 40-pin GPIO extension ribbon seats in a pocket from below. Its 2×20 pins sit in a recessed well 1 mm below the surface, so add-ons plug in from the top. The ribbon runs from the Pi's header up in front of the battery tray and twists under the crown into the plug, with at least 1.1 mm clear all the way. A 20 cm cable leaves slack. The Pi now sits with its GPIO header along the front edge, facing the eyes, and USB/Ethernet at the −X end.

      The handle is optional. bridge_handle.stl bolts onto the insert with the same 4 screw positions (M3×16 socket heads through bores in its posts). The grip arches 25 mm over the GPIO well, so add-ons stay usable with the handle on. Without it, the countersunk screws sit flush. The old twist-lock handle is retired, since the slot is now filled.

      New

      The cameras are the pupils

      pupil_ring.stl, printed in black, glues onto the front of each bezel hub around the lens. Its bore is a cone, 7.5 mm at the lens and 14.4 mm at the lip, so the lens reads as a deep pupil. It still leaves the camera a clear 153° field of view, and the lens clears the bore by 0.75 mm. Print the camera bezels in black too, so the spoke carrying the ribbon cable disappears into the dark screen.

      The screen graphics are built around the lens. The iris is a set of aperture blades that opens and closes with each expression and snaps briefly when the expression changes, like the camera refocusing. The pupil never moves off the lens: when the head looks around, the focus ring turns and the lids shift instead.

      New

      Battery tray, deck and bracket pads

      battery_tray.stl has three cradle ribs, two strap windows for 22 mm hook-and-loop straps, and a lead exit in the rear wall. It keeps at least 1.4 mm off the dome. battery_deck.stl has two 30 × 46 converter bays and a 40 × 20 charger bay, each with zip-tie slots, and screws to the tray from above. servo_bracket_L and _R_v2 add two 6 mm screw pads on top of each bracket, clear of the servo by 1.5 mm.

      Fixed

      Pi 5 standoffs cut from 6 to 4 mm

      pi_shelf.stl. The Pi mock now has a 16 mm USB/Ethernet block that overhangs the board edge by 2.5 mm. Even with that block at the end nearest a bracket, it clears by 1.4 mm. The servos are now 7.4 mm above the Pi's component block.

      Wiring

      Every connection is routed through the free space inside the head on a 2 mm grid. Each route keeps clear of every part and of the jaw's sweep up to 35°, and cables are routed one at a time so later ones go round earlier ones. Each finished cable was then rebuilt as a solid tube and intersected exactly with every part and every other cable. The last 6 mm at each end, where it plugs in, isn't counted. Round cables use their real diameter. Flat ribbons use a 4 mm envelope, as if folded, and the display cables use a 6 mm worst case sized for micro-HDMI.

      Fits

      All 14 cable runs fit, plus the GPIO ribbon

      No cable overlaps a part or another cable. The tightest gaps are the left display cable at 0.19 mm and the pack leads at 0.25 mm, both assuming the full 6 mm envelope; thinner cable gains room. The right camera ribbon wraps behind the rear edge of the Pi shelf and just touches it, which a flexible ribbon doesn't mind. "Buy" is the route length plus 15 % for bends and 30 mm at the connectors, rounded up to a stock length.

      Found

      The GPIO header was needed twice, so there's now a splitter

      The crown ribbon takes the whole 40-pin header, but the I2S amps need pins too. A 1-to-2 GPIO splitter board now sits on the Pi's header. Its upper header carries the ribbon's plug and a side header feeds the amps. That lifts the ribbon plug 12 mm; the ribbon's route was rechecked and still clears.

      New

      Three parts placed so the wiring has somewhere to go

      The DYNAMIXEL TTL interface (U2D2 size) lies on the left shelf wing, next to the Pi's USB ports. The two MAX98357A amps sit flat on the shelf in front of the Pi, just behind the speakers, so each speaker lead is only 23 mm and comes up through the speaker's vent hole. Both clear everything around them. These are placeholder shapes (_MOCK); stick them down with foam tape.

      Not wired

      Still to route once they exist

      The charge port and power switch, which have no holes in the skull yet, and the slip ring's fixed-side wires below the neck base. The endpoints on the Pi (USB-C, micro-HDMI, camera connectors) are placed from the Pi 5's published layout at board level. The last few millimetres into each plug aren't modelled.

      How this was measured

      Every part is loaded from its current STL and placed with the transforms the parts were built with (pivot axis on X, origin on the axis). The skull is skull, the jaw is jaw, and the right hub and bezel are the left ones turned 180°. Pairs are intersected with the Manifold kernel, which gives exact overlap volume and minimum gap.

      The ROBOTIS STEP mesh of the XL330 isn't watertight, so the servos were checked as a solid envelope: the case, the output horn and the idler disc. The viewer still shows the real servo model. The Pi 5, displays, cameras, speakers, cells and power boards are simple placeholder shapes (the _MOCK files), so clearances to them are only as good as those shapes.

      Overlaps under 0.01 mm³ were ignored as facet noise. The thrust pads were cut to hug the jaw's swept shape, and their outer edge touches the rotating jaw at the 14 µm level at a few angles.
